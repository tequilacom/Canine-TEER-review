1. Executive Summary

TEER (Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair) originates from the MitraClip technique in human medicine and has, over the past four years (2022–2026), become established as a frontier treatment direction in veterinary medicine, principally for canine degenerative mitral valve disease (MMVD). The only purpose-built device in this field is the canine V-Clamp, derived from the human ValveClamp originally developed by the team of Academician Junbo Ge at Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University; the team of Eric C. Orton at Colorado State University (CSU) has led the global clinical translation of the canine device. Published clinical data comprise approximately 111 dogs (CSU prospective cohort) + 20 (South Korea) + 4 (Thailand), with additional experimental work from Poland and clinical activity in China (Shanghai, Hong Kong) that has not yet been peer-reviewed.

Key evidence milestones: the CSU 50-dog feasibility study (2024, Front Vet Sci) → the CSU 111-dog follow-up with 2-year survival (2025, J Vet Cardiol: 6.3% in-hospital mortality, 78% 2-year cardiac survival) → the Korean 20-dog retrospective series (2025, JVIM: 35% early mortality, underscoring the importance of case selection) → a dense series of 2025–2026 publications on procedural standardization (dedicated TEE guidance), preoperative quantitative assessment (MR quantification, NT-proBNP/cTnI, cardiac CT), and case-selection frameworks. The focus of the field has shifted from "can it be done?" to "for whom, and how?"

Device lineage and geographic landscape: human ValveClamp (developed by the Ge Junbo team at Zhongshan Hospital; NMPA-approved 2023) → derived canine V-Clamp → validation in the CSU large cohort → adoption in South Korea, Thailand, Poland → first clinical use in the United Kingdom. Clinical activity in China (Shanghai, Hong Kong) has been ongoing since 2020, but international peer-reviewed publications remain absent (only one 2023 case report in a Chinese-language journal), representing a notable gap in the field's evidence chain.

3. Core Authors and Team Trajectories

1. Colorado State University (CSU) — Orton / Scansen / Potter team (the leading global clinical translators, largest case series)

Jan 2021 — First clinical use of transapical V-Clamp TEER (start of the 111-dog cohort). The canine V-Clamp is derived from the human ValveClamp (Zhongshan Hospital); the CSU team leads its veterinary clinical translation (device lineage detailed in item 7 below).
2021–2022 — Team foundation in valve surgery: Sutherland/Orton tricuspid repair (JVC 2021), Scansen structural interventions (VSD occlusion 2022), among others.
2024 — Prospective 50-dog feasibility study (Front Vet Sci, PMID 39717788): the first formal publication of TEER using a purpose-built canine device.
2025 — Three parallel lines: ① 111-dog follow-up with 2-year survival (JVC, PMID 41513577); ② MR quantification methodology (Del Nero, PMID 39914360); ③ collaborative review with human interventional cardiologist Carroll on translating human experience to dogs (PMID 41219030).
2026 — Dedicated TEE standardization paper (PMID 41656142) + cardiac CT valve imaging (Scansen, PMID 42270470). Trajectory: device-translation partnership → feasibility → large-cohort follow-up → standardization (imaging/methodology) → selection framework output.

2. Korea Animal Medical Center (Cheongju) — Lee Seung-Keun team

2025 — Retrospective 20-dog TEER series (JVIM, PMID 40459365, in collaboration with NC State): 35% early mortality, concentrated in leaflet injury/single-leaf attachment, comorbidities (tracheal collapse, hydrocephalus) and cardiogenic shock.
2025 — NT-proBNP/cTnI periprocedural safety prediction study (Vet Sci, PMID 40266932).
Trajectory — High-risk real-world data + biomarker-based risk stratification; in clear contrast to the highly selective CSU cohort, jointly identifying case selection as the decisive outcome variable.

3. Petchdee S team (Thailand)

2024 — Preliminary 4-dog report (Animals, PMID 39518790).
2026 — Myocardial work outcomes study (Vet Sci, PMID 42357795); team member Kongchatree published the "open repair vs TEER selection framework" review (see B-core). Trajectory: small-sample start → functional endpoints → decision-framework output.

4. Pasławska U team (Poland)

2026 — Transapical V-Clamp TEER study (Pol J Vet Sci, PMID 41860000) + translational review (10.5114/aic.2025.154190). Positioning: a bidirectional veterinary + experimental human medicine platform (new European entrant).

5. Uechi Masami team (Japan) — the comparator pathway

2025 — TEE safety study within a cardiopulmonary-bypass mitral repair programme (JVIM, PMID 40095386). Not performing TEER, but the central comparator team in the "open repair vs TEER" debate, and an output source of perioperative management evidence.

6. Viktor Szatmári (Utrecht University, the Netherlands)

In the multinational survey of MMVD treatment guideline utilization by van Staveren, Muis & Szatmári (Animals 2024;14:772, PMID 38473157), the Discussion contains the following statements on TEER:

  • "Interestingly, none of the respondents recommended surgical treatment at any of the disease stages, even though open heart surgery and transcatheter edge-to-edge repair techniques are available options for dogs with advanced stages of MMVD in Europe."
  • "Transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) is a promising new surgical treatment modality for selected dogs with MMVD. This technique is much less invasive and therefore less risky and more affordable than open heart surgery. However, there are several limiting factors, such as the size of the heart, the valve anatomy and valve pathology, why TEER cannot be performed in each dog with advanced MMVD."
  • "Also it is not yet clear in which disease stage TEER would offer the greatest clinical benefit. Long-term follow up information on a large number of dogs is required before evidence-based recommendation can be made for pet owners."

7. Device lineage: Zhongshan ValveClamp (human) → canine V-Clamp → international spread

2 Jul 2018 — The team of Academician Junbo Ge at Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University (Ge Junbo, Wang Chunsheng, Zhou Daxin, Wei Lai, Pan Wenzhi, Pan Cuizhen, et al.) performed the world's first ValveClamp transapical transcatheter edge-to-edge mitral repair (73-year-old woman with extreme MR). The original device concept came from the Zhongshan cardiac team. Compared with MitraClip: slimmer delivery (16F straight sheath), larger capture range, ultrasound-only guidance (radiation-free), simpler operation.
2019 — First-in-human results published in JACC Cardiovasc Interv (Pan W, Zhou D, Wu Y, Guo Y, …, Wei L, Ge J, Jilaihawi H, Leon MB; doi:10.1016/j.jcin.2019.05.043): 12 patients across three centres (Zhongshan/Fuwai/West China), procedural success 100%, all transapical.
2020 — Liu B, Leach SB, Pan Wenzhi (Zhongshan Hospital) et al., Front Vet Sci: a modified human ValveClamp used in 8 dogs with spontaneous MR, 100% success — the first canine TEER publication worldwide (PMID 33392290); also reference [60] in Szatmári's review.
Derivative — The purpose-built canine V-Clamp (derived from the human ValveClamp) → used by the CSU team for the 50/111-dog clinical studies; the UK Vet Times review explicitly documents this device lineage.
2023 — Twin milestones: ① the CLAMP-2 multicenter confirmatory trial published in JACC Cardiovasc Interv (Pan WZ et al.; 102 patients in 11 hospitals; acute procedural success 97%, 1-year effectiveness 87.3%, 12-month MR≤2+ in 97.8%; learning curve ~50 cases); ② on 7 Sep 2023 the ValveClamp received NMPA approval — the first domestically developed mitral interventional device approved in China (full human-literature list in Section 5).
2024–2025 — UK adoption: Willows Referrals (2024, among the first in Europe) → RVC QMHA + Bristol (announced Nov 2025), publicly citing the CSU 80% 2-year survival figure.

8. Chinese-speaking region (non-indexed clinical sources)

2020 → — Shanghai Shenpu Specialty Hospital: first minimally invasive mitral clip procedure in China (2020) → 2024 public reports of a radiation-free, contrast-free ultrasound-guided technique and multiple referral cases.
2023 — The first Chinese-language canine TEER case report: Ma Q, Wu JJ, Wang ZH, "A new method for treating canine mitral regurgitation" (Asian Case Reports in Veterinary Medicine 2023, doi:10.12677/acrpvm.2023.124005, Hans Publishers; not PubMed-indexed), citing the Zhongshan human ValveClamp first-in-human paper — the starting point of peer-reviewed records in the Chinese-language literature (although case and operator details are limited).
2026 — WeHUG, Hong Kong: first canine TEER in Hong Kong, with publicly stated exclusion criteria (severe valvular calcification/deformity). Clinical activity in the Chinese-speaking region is active, but international peer-reviewed publication remains absent.

4. Trends (Past 4 Years)

  • 1. From feasibility to scale: first formal clinical paper in 2024 (n=50) → largest cohort in 2025 (n=111, 2-year follow-up); 2-year cardiac survival ~78–80%, with safety data (6.3% in-hospital mortality) approaching early human M-TEER levels.
  • 2. Rapid internationalization: USA (CSU) → South Korea, Thailand (2024–2025) → Poland (2026) → clinical practice in the Chinese-speaking region (since 2020, unpublished). The V-Clamp remains the only canine-specific device with peer-reviewed data support.
  • 3. Evidence focus shifting to "selection and standardization": dense output in 2025–2026 of TEE procedural standards, MR quantification criteria, cardiac CT, biomarkers (NT-proBNP/cTnI) and two selection-framework reviews — case selection is consistently identified by all parties as the core variable (65% early survival in Korea vs 93.7% discharge survival at CSU).
  • 4. Systematic migration of human experience: the Orton×Carroll review (2025) establishes the translational paradigm "human RCT/registry experience → canine clinical pathways"; EVEREST-type anatomic selection logic is being adapted to dogs.
  • 5. Open questions: ① absence of prospective controlled trials versus medical therapy or surgery; ② insufficient consistency in MR reduction (post-procedural RF 30–35%); ③ the periprocedural complication spectrum (leaflet tear, single-leaf attachment, neurological complications) requires risk models; ④ high-impact international publications from the Chinese-speaking region remain absent (only one Chinese-language case report).
  • 6. China's dual role: China is both the device origin (Zhongshan ValveClamp → canine V-Clamp) and, since 2020, the site of pioneering clinical practice (Shanghai Shenpu, etc.), yet international peer-reviewed evidence shows a gap — a segment of the field that Chinese teams are well placed to complete.

5. Human ValveClamp (Transapical Edge-to-Edge Repair) Literature

Core investigators: Ge Junbo, Zhou Daxin, Pan Wenzhi (cardiology), Wei Lai (cardiac surgery), Pan Cuizhen, Li Wei, Long Yuliang (echocardiography). Prospective study of a multidisciplinary nursing model for transapical mitral clipping — a reference for team building.
Li S, Zhang H, Chen M, Wang Z, Lin Y
PMID 34321032
Registered trials (ongoing, no formal publication yet): CLAMP (NCT03869164; confirmatory follow-up to 2025); prospective multicenter RCT in functional MR (ChiCTR2400079688, registered 2024). Key milestones: world's first implant 2 Jul 2018 → FIM results in JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2019 → NMPA marketing approval on 7 Sep 2023 (the first domestically developed mitral interventional device in China). Its principle is homologous with MitraClip (surgical edge-to-edge suture), but the transapical access, ultrasound-only guidance and larger capture range constitute the technical basis for its translation into veterinary medicine (dogs).
